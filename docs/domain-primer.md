# Domain primer for builders

## Codes workflow vocabulary

**Acceptable solution:** a prescriptive route in the applicable code. **Alternative solution:** a proposed different route whose required performance must be demonstrated through the applicable process. **Jurisdiction:** the responsible local context and authority. **Attribution:** the relationship of a code provision to objectives and functional statements. **Completeness:** required information appears in a package. **Adequacy:** evidence and analysis actually support the case; presence alone cannot establish this.

Store code references as structured tuples: document, edition, printing, division/part/article, source location and applicability status. Never concatenate a model-generated article number into a verified citation. Have domain reviewers establish the actual checklist and professional involvement triggers.

## Carbon workflow vocabulary

**Quantity takeoff:** deriving material or assembly quantities from design information. **Bill of quantities:** structured line items, units and quantities. **EPD:** an environmental declaration with an explicit product, method, declared unit and scope. **A1–A3:** product-stage modules; retain the declared boundary. **GWP:** global warming potential, reported with an explicit indicator and method. **Intensity:** scoped emissions divided by an explicitly defined floor area. **Coverage:** what the estimate includes relative to the intended building elements.

An EPD per m2 of insulation at a declared thermal resistance cannot safely be treated as a factor per arbitrary m2 of any insulation thickness. A per-kg factor needs a justified mass conversion. Compare equivalent function and performance before ranking substitutions. Keep fossil, biogenic and land-use indicators distinct when the underlying declaration separates them.

## Common measurement traps

- Millimetres mistaken for metres: volume can be wrong by orders of magnitude.
- Plan area mistaken for sloped roof area.
- Gross wall area used without a consistent openings policy.
- Slab, footings and foundation walls overlapping or counted twice.
- Assembly layers missing or duplicated.
- Nominal lumber dimensions treated as measured finished dimensions.
- A mirrored plan reused without checking changed elevations or schedules.
- Operational-energy data presented as embodied-carbon data.
- A single assembly subtotal presented as whole-building performance.

Use the [NRC guidance](https://nrc-publications.canada.ca/eng/view/object/?id=f7bd265d-cc3d-4848-a666-8eeb1fbde910), source declarations, and qualified reviewers to establish the methodology your actual product needs.
